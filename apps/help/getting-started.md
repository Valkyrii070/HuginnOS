# Getting started
The desktop, windows and where things are.

## Open an app
Press Super (the Windows key), or click the round orb at the left end of the dock, for the start menu: your recent apps, every app (it scrolls; the pills along the top show one group), and Lock, Sleep, Restart, Shut Down and Sign Out at the bottom. Type a few letters to find an app; press Tab to search everything instead. Right-click an app (here, in the dock or in Search) for its own island: its recent files, things it can do, and Keep in the dock.

## The dock
An open app has a bar under its tile for each of its windows (up to three; a dim one when they're all hidden). Click an app that isn't open and a light in its colour runs round its tile until its window is there. Click the app in front to hide it (it folds into its tile), and click again to bring it back. Point at an app with several windows, or click it, for its windows as an island; middle-click opens a new window, scrolling goes through them, and dropping a file on an app opens it there.

At the right end of the apps, **Downloads** is a stack: the newest files piled up, a mark on it while something's still arriving. Click it for the list, drag a file out, or right-click a file to open it, show it in Files, copy, rename or move it to the Bin. Drag any folder onto the end of the dock to keep it there as another stack. How it looks, how big the dock is and what clicking does are in Settings › Desktop & Dock.

With more than one screen, rest the pointer at the bottom of another screen for a moment and the dock comes there (Settings › Desktop & Dock › Moves to the screen you're using). The Overview spreads out every screen's windows on that screen.

## Search everything
Press Super+Space, or click the magnifier in the top bar. Type, and apps, files, Settings pages, sums ("84.60 × 15%", "5 km in mi") and Huginn's open tabs come up; Enter opens the top one, Tab narrows it to one kind. Everything is searched on this computer; only "Search the web" sends what you typed anywhere. Before you type, it offers what you were doing last: the file you had open, the song you paused.

## The live island
At the right end of the dock, an island appears while something is going on: music or a video playing (in any app, Netflix in Huginn included), a timer in Clock, an install in App Store, files arriving in Downloads, an event starting soon, a call on your phone. When several things happen at once, the most important gets the island and up to two more wait beside it as round bubbles; click a bubble to swap it in, or scroll over the island. With two things playing, the island shows the one whose window isn't in front.

Point at the island for a moment and it opens, with all its controls and everything else going on; move away and it closes. Click it to go to the app; right-click for the app's own island. Calls and timers that go off open it by themselves. The play/pause key works on whatever the island shows.

When an app uses your screen, camera or microphone (sharing your screen in a call, say), the island says so for a few seconds, then it stays as a bubble for as long as it's on, with a coloured dot in the top bar: red for the screen, green for the camera, orange for the microphone. Those can't be hidden.

## Move and size a window
Huginn windows have no title bar. Drag a window by its top strip or by its name island in the top-left corner (or anywhere, holding Super). Drag the bottom-right corner to resize it, and double-click the top strip to make it fill the screen.

## Keep a window on top
Super+Ctrl+T (or the app's menu › Window › Keep on Top) keeps the window in front over every other window, even when you click another; press it again to stop. A video's picture-in-picture window from the browser stays on top and on every space by itself.

## Snap windows side by side
Drag a window to the left or right edge (or press Super+← / Super+→) and it fills that half; keep pressing for a third and two thirds. Drag it to the top, or Super+↑, to fill the screen, and to a corner for a quarter. When one half is taken, the other half offers your other windows: pick one to fill it. Hold the pointer on a window's green dot for all the layouts, halves, thirds and quarters, at once. Super+Z opens Layouts for the window in front: pick a part of any screen for it (halves, thirds, the middle third, two thirds, quarters, top and bottom). Below them are window sets: Keep These Windows remembers where every window is on every screen under a name (Gaming, Writing), and Put Back puts them all there again later, starting the apps that aren't open.

## Screen corners and the screen saver
Settings › Desktop & Dock › Screen corners gives each corner something to do when you push the pointer into it: the Overview, the desktop, the start menu, the Control Centre, Today, Search, Look Up, the screen saver or locking. Only the outer corners of your screens count. Settings › Appearance › Screen saver shows the time, your photos or the realm's scene after the computer is left alone for a while, before the screen dims, never over a film or a game; any key or movement ends it.

## The Overview and spaces
Press Super+Tab (or swipe up with three fingers) and your windows glide apart so you can see them all: click one to go to it. Along the top are your spaces, separate desktops: + adds one, and dragging a window onto a space moves it there. Super+Ctrl+← / → switches space (or three fingers left and right), Super+1 to 9 goes straight to one, and Super+Shift+← / → takes the window in front with you.

## Close, hide or fill the screen
The three dots in the top-left island close the window (red), hide it (yellow) and fill the screen (green). The controls for each app float in islands around its edges.

## Light, dark and colour
Settings changes between light and dark and picks the realm, the colour the whole desktop leans towards. Every app keeps its own colour on top of that, so you can tell them apart at a glance. Apps that aren't Huginn's follow along too: their colours, dark or light and the font change with yours.

## Quick settings and notifications
The control centre slides out from the top bar on the right: Wi‑Fi and Bluetooth (tap the round switch to turn them on or off, the name to open Settings), Do Not Disturb, night light, dark, brightness and sound (tap the speaker to mute), and the notifications that came, with Clear. Under the sound slider, Apps shows everything playing sound with its own slider and mute, so a game can be quieter than the call beside it. History shows every notification of the last week, missed or dismissed, grouped by day; Clear all empties the list and leaves the history (Settings › Notifications › Keep a history).

A notification blooms out of the top right with its app's colour running round its edge. Several from one app pile up; click the pile to spread it. Messages and mail can be answered right there. While something fills the screen, or you're sharing it, they wait quietly (calls and alarms still come through).

## The app's menu
Every app's settings, help and commands are in one place: click the app's name in the top bar (or press Alt on its own). At the top are a search box (type to find any command), About and Settings; then the app's own groups, File, Edit, View and so on, each opening to the side; Open Recent cascades too. With nothing open, the name says Huginn and the menu is the desktop's own. If you'd rather have the groups along the bar, Settings › Desktop & Dock › App menus has "A row of menus".

## Today
Click the date in the top bar, or press Super+T: the month, the weather, what's coming and a few clocks, as islands. Type straight away to add something: "lunch with Astrid friday 12" becomes an event and "remind me to call Sam at 6pm" a reminder; a preview shows what it understood before you press Enter.

## Get more apps
App Store installs apps from Flathub. Web apps you install from the Huginn browser appear alongside the others.

## Switch windows and keys to know
Hold Alt and press Tab to see your open windows; let go of Alt to go to the one picked (Shift+Tab goes back, Escape stays put). Super on its own opens the start menu, Super+Tab the Overview, Super+T Today, Alt on its own the app's menu, Super+Space searches everything, Super+L locks, Super+Ctrl+Space switches keyboard layout (when you have more than one), Super+Alt+S turns the screen reader on or off, Print saves a screenshot of the whole screen to Pictures › Screenshots, and Shift+Print (or Super+Shift+S) opens Screenshot to choose what to take. Clicking an open app in the dock brings its window forward instead of opening another.

Super+E opens Files, Super+I Settings and Ctrl+Alt+T Terminal. Super+D puts every window out of the way to show the desktop, and brings them back when pressed again. With more than one screen, Super+Alt+← / → moves the window in front to the screen beside it, snapped or full as it was. Settings › Keyboard Shortcuts lists them all: change any of them, turn one off, or make your own to open an app or run a command.

## Emoji and symbols
Press Super+. (full stop) wherever you're typing: type a word to find one (heart, party, arrow, euro), use the arrows or the pointer, and Enter types it in. The ones you used are first next time; the hands at the bottom set the skin tone. Whatever you'd copied stays on the clipboard.

## Look Up anything on the screen
Press Super+A: the screen holds still. Circle or drag over anything, or click on some words, and they're read on this computer. Copy Text, Translate, Search the Web, Copy or Save the picture, or ask Huginn AI about it (when it's on). Esc starts again, Esc twice closes it.

## Talk, and it types
Press Super+H wherever you're typing and speak. Pause for a moment, or press Super+H again, and what you said is typed in; the live island shows that it's listening, with Done and Cancel. It all happens on this computer: the sound never leaves it. The first time, choose a speech model in Settings › Keyboard & Mouse › Dictation (Quick, Balanced or Accurate); it's downloaded once. With Tidy it up on there, Huginn AI makes what you said into clean text first: punctuation and paragraphs, without the ums and false starts, and lists where you listed things. Automatic language works out what you're speaking each time, or choose one there.

## Clipboard history
Super+V shows what you copied lately, text and pictures; pick one and it's pasted where you were typing. Type to find something, pin what you use often, Delete removes one. It's off until you turn it on (the first Super+V asks, or Settings › Privacy). Passwords copied from Passwords and Authenticator are never kept.

## When an app asks
Some things apps can only do by asking you first, in a card of Huginn's own: using the camera or taking screenshots ("Allow …?", answered once), which app should open a file or a link (**Open With**, the last one you used already picked), or a colour from the screen (the screen holds still with a magnifier: click the colour). An app that asks for a screenshot holds the screen still too: drag over the part to share, or take the whole screen; Escape says no.

## Open and Save windows
Whenever any app asks for a file, Huginn's own window comes up: your places down the left, the folder as a list or as pictures, a path along the top to click back along, and search. Saving, type a name (its ending follows the type you pick), make a new folder there, and Huginn asks before replacing anything. Ctrl+H shows hidden files. It remembers how you like to look and where each app last saved.

## The preview session
At the sign-in screen, the session menu has "Huginn OS (own compositor, preview)": the same desktop, drawn by Huginn's own compositor instead of KWin. It's being built in steps: snapping, the Overview, spaces, windows folding into the dock, soft shadows, rounded corners, frosted glass behind the bar, dock and menus, and a Huginn title island for apps that don't draw their own all work there; screenshots and screen sharing for apps still need the "Huginn" session. Apps made for X11 open in it like any other, and a game that fills the screen is shown directly, with FreeSync on screens that have it. If anything misbehaves, sign out and pick "Huginn" again. If the lock screen ever won't take your password, switch to a text console (Ctrl+Alt+F3), sign in there and type `loginctl unlock-sessions`.
