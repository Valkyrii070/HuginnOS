# Settings
Everything about this computer, on one page each. Search at the top right finds any setting.

## Find a setting
Type in Search settings: "night light", "password", "VPN". Pick a result to go straight to its page. Each page has its own colour, and the window takes it on.

## Wi‑Fi and network
Turn Wi‑Fi on or off, pick a network and type its password once (the system keeps it). The eye at the end of a password box shows what you've typed, so you can check it; click it again to hide it. Join another network covers ones that hide their name. This network shows your IP address, router and DNS; Change… by DNS lets you use servers of your own (Quad9 or Cloudflare, say), or leave it empty for the network's own. Add a VPN from the WireGuard (.conf) or OpenVPN (.ovpn) file your provider gives you. Personal hotspot shares a wired connection with your phone; Settings tells you the password it made.

## Bluetooth
Put the device in pairing mode (usually by holding its button), choose Look for devices, then Pair. My devices shows what's paired, whether it's connected and its battery.

## Displays
A screen you plug in starts at its own resolution and its fastest refresh rate, with a size of things to suit how sharp it is. With two screens or more, drag them on the map to match your desk, and choose the main one (it has the dock and the widgets; every screen has a top bar, unless you turn that off in Desktop & Dock). Identify puts each screen's name on it for a moment, so you can tell which is which. When a screen is unplugged, turned off or drops away while asleep, its windows move to the main screen, and go back when it returns. For each screen: resolution, size of things (scaling), refresh rate, turning, and Show: its own part of the desktop, or the same as another screen (for a projector or a TV). Under Drawing, Glass sets how the frosted glass is drawn (Light is quicker, for many fast screens or slower graphics), and Frame times shows how smoothly each screen draws, in its top-right corner (Super+Alt+F). Brightness has a slider for a laptop's own screen and one for each monitor that lets the computer change it over the cable (DDC/CI: if a monitor has no slider, turn DDC/CI on in its own menu). Night light makes colours warmer in the evening: from sunset to sunrise (worked out from your time zone's city, so nothing asks where you are), at set hours, or always.

## Sound
Choose where sound plays and which microphone listens, and how loud each is. Alerts: the alert sound (what an app's bell plays) and how loud it is, and a click when the volume keys change the volume. Apps playing now lets you turn one app down without touching the rest.

## Battery and power
The ring shows the charge; under it, the battery's health (how much it holds compared with new) and its charge cycles. Power mode trades speed for battery. Choose when the screen turns off and the computer sleeps, plugged in and on battery, and what closing the lid and the power button do.

## Keyboard shortcuts
Every one of Huginn's shortcuts, grouped: Change, then press the new keys (Escape leaves it as it was, Backspace turns it off). A key already used by another shortcut moves to the new one, and Settings says which lost it. Under "Your own", choose an app or type a command and press its keys. Reset All puts Huginn's back as they came. **Hold Super to see them all** (on at first): hold Super on its own for a moment and every shortcut shows, grouped, until you let go; press one while it shows and it works as usual.

## Keyboard and mouse
Add keyboard layouts and switch between them with Super+Ctrl+Space, or by clicking the letters in the top bar (they show when there's more than one). Make Caps Lock another Control or Escape, choose whether Num Lock is on when you sign in, turn on the Compose key, and set how keys repeat. Each touchpad and mouse has its own speed, acceleration (off for games: the pointer goes exactly as far as you move), scroll speed, natural scrolling, tap to click, how a touchpad right-clicks (two fingers, or its bottom-right corner) and left-handed buttons.

## Date, time and language
Pick your time zone and whether time servers keep the clock right. Language sets what the system speaks; Formats sets how dates, numbers and money look, with a sample underneath. A language the computer doesn't have yet is added first (it asks for the administrator password). Language changes take effect the next time you sign in; Huginn's own apps are in English for now, other apps follow it where they're translated.

## Users
Change your picture (Huginn draws some from your initials, or pick a photo), your name and your password. Sign in by itself skips the password when the computer starts (only for a computer at home: anyone who switches it on gets in; the screen still locks when you leave it). Administrators can add people: they choose their own password the first time they sign in. The system asks for your password before adding or removing anyone.

## Default apps
Each tile is a kind of file: click it to choose which app opens it. Open when you sign in lists apps that start by themselves.

## Printers and scanners
Printers on your network that need no driver (nearly all made since 2015) appear by themselves while they're switched on. Use First picks the one apps print to unless you choose; Add a printer by its address is for one on another network. Scanners lists what Scan can use. Printing and scanning themselves are in Print & Scan.

## Privacy and security
The firewall keeps everything outside from reaching this computer unless you allow it. What apps may use lists the camera, microphone, location and more that apps from Flathub have asked for: switch any off, or Reset to be asked again. Screen lock: whether the screen locks by itself, after how long without use (the screen can turn off sooner and stay unlocked until then), and whether it locks before the computer sleeps. Turning off Remember recent files stops Huginn's apps and others from noting what you open. Empty the Trash by itself deletes what's been in the Trash longer than you choose. Clipboard history is here, off until you turn it on.

## Sign-in screen
Put widgets beside the time on the sign-in screen: Today (your next events), Weather, This PC (updates, downloads that finished, the battery) and Now Playing (what last played). Add them, choose small, wide or tall, and drag them in the picture to change their order. Each has a Before sign-in switch: on, it shows what your desktop last saw, for anyone at the computer to read; off, it shows only its name and a lock. Today and Now Playing start off. Nobody at the sign-in screen can change them; only you, here, once you've signed in. The realm the sign-in screen wears is here too (it's everyone's, so it asks for an administrator's password).

## Notifications
Do Not Disturb holds every banner and sound; quiet hours turn it on by itself at night. Show what a notification says: Never keeps banners to the app's name (the Control Centre still shows it all); When unlocked hides it while the screen is locked. Notification sounds, and each app's three switches: allowed, banners, sounds.

## Where apps open
In Desktop & Dock, Where apps open: choose an app, then the screen its windows open on, the space, and whether they fill the screen or stay on top of other windows.

## Appearance and wallpaper
Light or dark, and the realm. Each realm has its own living scene for a wallpaper: Aurora's northern lights,
Bifröst's bridge of light, Muspelheim's fire and Niflheim's ice. It moves on the sign-in screen and comes to rest
after you sign in. Turn on **Live wallpaper** to keep it moving on the desktop too (it uses a little more power, and
stays still when Reduce motion is on). **Sign-in screen** shows which realm everyone sees before signing in; **Use …**
changes it to yours, after the administrator password. **On the desktop** can show **A picture** of your own instead:
pick one of your newest from Pictures, or Other… for any. It's copied into Huginn's own folder, so moving or deleting
the original doesn't take it away; Realm's scene brings the realm back. **Accent colour** under the realms is the
realm's own, or **From wallpaper**: the colour your wallpaper is most about, made bright enough (or deep enough, in
light mode) for the words on it to read. It changes when the wallpaper does; a wallpaper with too little colour keeps
the realm's. Each app still keeps its own colour.

## Live wallpapers you make
Appearance › Wallpaper › **Made by you** › **Make one**. Start from a realm's scene in colours of your own (click a
colour, then pick one), a picture of yours (drifting slowly, breathing in and out, or still) or a video of yours
(it loops without sound). Add any of snow, rain, stars, fireflies, lights, leaves and embers on top, then choose how
many, their colour and the speed. The preview at the top plays as you go; the Ideas row starts you off. Save and Use
puts it on the desktop. Click one to use it, or its pencil to change it. **Share…** saves it as a .huginn-wallpaper file
in Documents to give someone, and **Add from a file…** puts one you were given in your list. Live wallpapers rest
while a window fills the screen (a game, a film), and stay still with Live wallpaper off or Reduce motion on.

## Icons and the pointer
Appearance › **Icons**: Huginn's own puts every app on smoked glass; Breeze, Breeze Dark and elementary change apps'
and folders' icons everywhere (some apps show them after they're opened again). **Find More** opens gnome-look.org;
download an icon theme there (a .tar.gz or .tar.xz), then **Add icons from a file…**. Ones you added can be removed.
**Change an app's icon…** gives any app a picture of your own, on Huginn's glass or just as it is; **Its Own Again**
puts its icon back.

Appearance › **Pointer**: each pointer style shows its arrow, hand, text, waiting and moving pointers; click one to use
it, and choose its size. Pointer styles you download are added the same way as icons.

## Accessibility
Seeing: the screen reader (Orca) reads out what's on the screen (Super+Alt+S turns it on and off from anywhere); Zoom magnifies around the pointer (follow it, keep it centred, or push at the edges); Make everything bigger; High contrast; Solid surfaces instead of glass; Reduce motion; colour filters for colour blindness, or grey; and the pointer's size. Hearing: flash every screen white for a moment instead of playing the alert sound. **Live captions** (Super+Ctrl+L, or Live captions in the Control Centre) writes out what's playing, a video, a call or a game, in a dark bar over everything; drag it anywhere and it stays there, and hover it for its settings or to close it. It all happens on this computer with one of dictation's speech models (Settings › Keyboard & Mouse › Dictation; Quick keeps up best), in dictation's language; the sound never leaves the computer and nothing is kept. Choose the text size, the model when you have more than one, or Put Back the bar. Moving and typing: sticky keys (shortcuts one key at a time), slow keys (a key must be held a moment), bounce keys (ignore quick repeats), moving the pointer with the number keys, and the on-screen keyboard.

## The computer's name
About › Name › Rename… sets what other devices see (over Bluetooth, on the network). The network name is made from it: "Astrid's Desk" is astrids-desk.

## Sync
Settings › Sync keeps your Notes, Reminders, Calendar, Contacts and Huginn OS's settings the same on all your computers, together with Huginn's Favorites, Reading List, passwords and open tabs. There's no account: your devices meet in a folder you choose, such as one Syncthing, Nextcloud or Dropbox keeps in step, a network share, or a USB stick, and everything in it is sealed so only your devices can read it. Start Syncing on the first computer; on the others, choose the same folder and paste the pairing key from Add a Device. A note changed on two computers at once keeps both versions; settings that belong to one computer, like its screens, stay with it. Stop Syncing leaves everything that's on the computer as it is.

## A problem report
About › Make a Problem Report gathers what helps someone find out what went wrong (the logs, the hardware, crashes) into one .zip in Documents. Names and addresses are left out; open it to see everything that's in it before you send it.
