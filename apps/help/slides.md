# Slides
Presentations, with the freedom of a canvas: put text, shapes and pictures anywhere on a slide.

## Making slides
Ctrl+M adds a slide like the one you're on; the small arrow beside + in the filmstrip lets you pick its layout (title, title and text, section, two columns, title only, blank). Layout at the top changes the slide in front: its placeholders move, and nothing you've written is lost. Drag slides in the filmstrip to reorder them; right-click one to duplicate, hide or delete it.

## Text, shapes and pictures
The top island adds a text box, a shape (rectangles, ovals, stars, arrows, speech bubbles, lines…) or a picture. Click an item to choose it, click again to type in it, or just start typing. Drag to move it: pink guides show when it lines up with the slide's middle and edges or with other items, and Shift keeps the move straight. The round handles size it (pictures keep their shape unless you hold Shift) and the handle above turns it (Shift: in steps of 15°). The arrow keys nudge what's chosen; Shift nudges further. Drag across empty space to choose several.

## Tables and charts
The table button (a small grid) or Insert › Table: point at the size you want. The chart button (four bars) or Insert › Chart: columns, bars, lines or a pie. Double-click either (or Edit the Cells / Edit the Data) to type in it: + and − add and take away rows and columns; a table can have a heading row, stripes, its colour and its text size, and a chart a title and a key. In a chart each column after the first is a series and the first column holds the labels. Both go to PowerPoint as its own tables and charts and come back from it.

## Formatting
While something is chosen, the top island shows its formatting: font, size, bold, italic, underline, colour, alignment, bullets and numbering (Tab and Shift+Tab change a bullet's level), and for shapes their fill, outline and kind. Arrange brings items forward or back, lines them up and spreads them out.

## Themes and backgrounds
The palette button picks a theme (each with its colours and fonts) and the slide size (16:9 or 4:3). A slide's background can be the theme's, a colour or a picture, for one slide or all of them.

## Transitions and builds
Transition sets how a slide comes on (fade, push, wipe, zoom or cover) and how long it takes. Build, on a chosen item, makes it come in on a click while presenting: appearing, fading, rising or zooming, and for text one paragraph at a time.

## Presenting
F5 starts from the beginning, Shift+F5 from this slide. Click, Space or the arrow keys go on (and back); B blanks the screen black and W white; type a slide's number and Enter to jump; Esc stops. With a second screen, the show goes there and this screen shows the presenter view: the slide, what comes next, your notes, a timer and the clock. Alt+F5 opens the presenter view on one screen, to rehearse.

## Other suites' files
Slides opens PowerPoint (.pptx) files with their slides, design, text formatting, shapes, pictures, tables, notes, transitions and builds, and says what didn't come across. OpenDocument (.odp) and old PowerPoint (.ppt) files open too. Export makes PowerPoint, PDF (with text you can select) and OpenDocument files.
