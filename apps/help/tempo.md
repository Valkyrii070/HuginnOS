# Tempo
Make music: instruments, drummers, loops, recording and a mixer.

## Starting a song
Pick a template: Songwriter (piano, bass, drummer), Beat Maker, Band, Voice and Podcast, or an empty song. Tempo saves as you go; each song is a folder in Music/Tempo, with its recordings, so it can be copied whole.

## Tracks and regions
Each track is an instrument, a drummer or a recording. Its regions are the pieces of music on it: drag them to move, drag their ends to make them longer or shorter. Ctrl+D copies the picked region; Ctrl+T cuts it at the playhead; Delete takes it away.

## Playing and recording
Space plays and stops; Return goes back to the start. R records: notes from the keyboard at the bottom, your computer's keyboard (Ctrl+K, then A to ; are the white keys and W, E, T, Y, U, O, P the black ones), a MIDI keyboard plugged in, or your microphone on an audio track. C turns the cycle on, to go round a part again and again; K turns the metronome on and off.

## The piano roll
Double-click a region to open it. Click to add a note, drag to move it, drag its end to change how long it lasts; how hard it's played is the bar underneath. Quantize puts notes on the beat.

## Drums
A drummer plays by itself: drag on the pad: further right plays busier, higher hits harder; pick a style (pop, rock, hip hop, house, funk, trap) and a kit. A fill comes every fourth bar. For your own beat, the drum grid has a row for each drum and a step for each sixteenth.

## Loops
The Loops drawer has chord progressions, bass lines, beats and melodies to start from. Click one to hear it, drag it onto the song to use it. They follow the song's key and speed.

## Instruments and smart controls
Fifteen instruments: pianos, organ, pads, strings, leads, basses, guitar, brass and three drum kits. The smart controls shape each one with a few knobs (brightness, attack, release and so on), and each track can have effects: EQ, compressor, drive, chorus, delay and filter.

## The mixer
A fader, pan, mute and solo for every track, with meters. Each track sends as much as you like to the shared reverb and echo. Press A to show volume automation: click on a track's line to add a point, drag it, double-click to take it away. A limiter on the output stops the mix clipping.

## Sharing
Export makes WAV, FLAC, MP3, AAC or Ogg in Music/Tempo Exports, or stems (a file for each track) for another studio, or a MIDI file of the notes. MIDI files and sounds can be brought in, too: drop them on the window.

## Sections
Mark the parts of your song with + above the bars: Intro, Verse, Chorus and so on, named as you go (double-click one to rename it, drag its end to make it longer). Pick a section to copy it straight after itself, move it earlier or later, or delete it with its music (the rest of the song closes up). Removing a marker keeps the music.

## Stacks
Ctrl-click the tracks you want together, then press Stack: they play into one stack with its own effects, fader and sends, shown above them. Click the stack's icon to fold its tracks away. Any track can be sent into a stack from Smart Controls (Plays Into).

## Drawing any knob over time
Press A for automation lanes. Click the name under a track to pick what its lane draws: volume, pan, the reverb and echo sends, or any knob of its effects (a filter opening up, a delay getting wetter). Click to add a point, drag it, double-click to take it away.

## Ducking
Duck Under… in Smart Controls makes a track dip whenever another plays: the bass under the kick, the music under a voice. Any compressor can listen to another track the same way (Listens To).

## Editing recordings
Pick a recording and the Region bench opens: gain, fades in and out, reverse, transpose (the timing stays), and follow the song's tempo (the pitch stays).

## Takes and comping
Turn the cycle on (C) and record: every time round becomes a take. Click a take to hear it, or drag across the best part of each take to comp them together. Keep This and Flatten lets the other takes go.

## The sampler
Drop any sound on Instruments (or on Smart Controls) and play it across the keys: Tempo hears its note, so it stays in tune. Choose Held or One Shot, and trim its start and end. Drop a sound on a drum's name in the drum grid to play your own sound on that drum.

## Arpeggiator and chords
In Smart Controls, the arpeggiator plays held notes one at a time, in time with the song (up, down, up and down, as played or random, over one to three octaves). The chord trigger turns one finger into a chord: a fixed shape, or the chords of the song's key.

## Groove
Pick a MIDI region and open the region pane: Swing pushes every other eighth (or sixteenth) late, from straight at 50 % to a triplet feel at 66 % and beyond; Humanize moves each note a little off the grid and a little softer or harder, the same way each time. The notes stay where you wrote them; it's how they're played.

## Freezing and bouncing
A track heavy with plugins can be frozen (Smart Controls): its sound is drawn once and played from the file, light on the computer, with its fader, pan and sends still working. Change anything it depends on and it plays live again until you freeze it again. Bounce in Place puts the same sound on an audio track of its own beside it and mutes the original.

## Putting a recording in tune
Pick a recording, then Find the Notes: every note sung or played is found and drawn over the line as it was sung. Tune to the Key moves them all toward the song's key by as much as you like; drag a note to put it exactly on a pitch, double-click it to let it follow the key again. Make MIDI puts the notes on an instrument track.

## Drums to notes
Pick a drum recording and choose Drums to Notes: its kick, snare and hi-hat are heard (even when they're hit together) and written as notes on a drum track under it, as hard as they were hit. Pick any kit for them, double a thin kick, or mute the recording and keep only the notes. Check them: a hi-hat under a loud snare can hide.

## Finding the chords
Find the Chords (on a recording, or on a region of notes) works out its chords beat by beat, major and minor, and shows them over the region; click one to go to it. Play the Chords puts them on a pad on a new track under it, a low root and the chord held for each: a start for strings, or a bass line.

## Mastering
Beside the Mixer, Master My Song listens to the whole song and sets the master to it: its balance, its width, a gentle compressor, and as loud as where it's going (streaming, loud, podcast), never past −1 dB. Turn it off to hear the difference.
A reference song is one that sounds the way you'd like yours to: choose one, and Master Like It moves your balance toward it and makes your song as loud. Hear It in Place of Your Song plays the reference where your song is, turned to be just as loud, so you can compare what's left fairly.

## Scoring a film
Drop a film on Tempo (or Export › Score a Film…) and it's the song's picture: the Picture window shows it where the playhead is, its sound comes in on a track of its own, and −¼ s and +¼ s move where in the film the song starts. Ctrl+Shift+P hides and shows the window. Export › The Film, With the Music saves the film with your song under it (the picture as it is, not made again).
