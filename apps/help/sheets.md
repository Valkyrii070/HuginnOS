# Sheets
Tables, sums, budgets and charts, with the keys and formulas you know from Excel.

## Typing
Click a cell and type; Enter goes down, Tab goes right, and Enter after a row of Tabs goes back to where the row began. F2 or a double-click changes what's in a cell. Dates, times, percentages and money are recognised as you type them (1/3/2026, 14:30, 12%, $4.50).

## Formulas
Start a cell with = to calculate, for example =SUM(B2:B9). As you type, the functions that match are listed (Tab takes the first). After an operator or a bracket, click or drag over cells, or use the arrows, to put their names in; each reference is outlined in its own colour. Sheets knows over a hundred of Excel's functions: sums and counts with conditions, lookups (XLOOKUP, VLOOKUP, INDEX and MATCH), text, dates, rounding and money (PMT, NPV, IRR).

## Choosing cells
Drag, or Shift with a click or the arrows. Ctrl+arrows jump to the edge of the data, Ctrl+A chooses the table (twice: everything), Ctrl+Space a column, Shift+Space a row. Click a letter or number for a whole column or row. With several cells chosen, their sum, average and count show beside the formula.

## Filling
Drag the small square at the corner of the chosen cells to carry them on: numbers and dates continue their series (1, 2, 3… or Jan, Feb, Mar…), formulas follow along. Double-click the square to fill down as far as the column beside it goes. Ctrl+D fills down, Ctrl+R fills right.

## Formatting
The island at the bottom: bold, italic, underline, colours, alignment, wrapping, number formats (number, currency, percent, dates, times, scientific) and decimal places. Drag the line between two column letters to resize, double-click it to fit.

## Sorting and filtering
A↓ and Z↓ sort the table by the column you're in (a heading row stays put). The filter button puts arrows on the heading row: choose which values show. Freeze (❄) keeps the top row, the first column, or everything above and left of the chosen cell in view.

## Highlight cells
The button with three coloured lines (or Format › Highlight cells) colours cells by what's in them: greater or less than a number, between two, equal to, text that contains some words, duplicates, the top or bottom few, above or below the average, empty cells and errors. Colour scales shade a range from low to high, and data bars draw a bar in each cell. The first rule that fits a cell colours it; change their order or take one off in the same panel. They go to Excel and come back from it.

## Pivot tables
Choose a table with names of its columns along the top (or a cell in it), then Data › Pivot table: a row for each name in one column, a column for each name in another if you like, and the sum, count, average, smallest or largest of a third, on a new sheet. Its numbers follow the table; Refresh brings in names added since.

## Charts
Choose the cells with the numbers (and their labels), then the chart button: columns, bars, lines, area or a pie. Click a chart to move it, resize it from its corner, change its kind, title and cells, or delete it.

## Sheets in a file
The tabs at the bottom left: + adds a sheet; click the one in front to rename, duplicate or delete it. Formulas can use other sheets (=Costs!B4).

## Choices and limits
Right-click › Choices for These Cells… (or Data › Choices for cells) sets what may go in the cells you chose: a list to pick from (type the choices with commas between them, or name the cells that hold them, such as E1:E5), whole numbers, numbers, dates or a length of text, between two values, at least, at most or exactly one. A cell with a list shows an arrow when you're on it: click it, or press Alt+↓, and pick. Anything else is turned away, or let in with a warning if you'd rather.

## Notes
Right-click › Add a Note… (or Insert › Note…) puts a few words on a cell; a red corner shows it has one, and resting the pointer on the cell shows the note. Notes move with their rows and columns, and when sorting.

## Other suites' files
Sheets opens and saves Excel (.xlsx) with formulas, formats, frozen panes, filters, charts, choices (data validation) and notes, and tells you what didn't come across. OpenDocument (.ods) and old Excel (.xls) files open too. Export makes CSV and PDF.
